6.1 Purpose of the Control Unit
The control unit interprets the decoded instruction and generates the control signals required by the datapath. These signals determine operand selection, ALU operation, memory access, write-back source, branch behavior, and register updates. Once generated in the Decode stage, the control signals are propagated through the pipeline registers until they are no longer required.

Why generate all control signals in Decode? Decode is the only stage that interprets the instruction, avoiding repeated decoding downstream.
Why use fixed-width encoded signals? Encoded control buses reduce wiring and make interfaces scalable compared with individual one-hot control lines.
Why keep reserved encodings? They support future ISA extensions without altering the module interfaces.

Why are encoded control signals used?
Decision
Operations such as ALU functions and branch types are represented using encoded control signals rather than individual one-hot control lines.
Why?
Encoded signals reduce the number of wires between modules and simplify the interfaces. They also provide reserved encodings for future ISA extensions without modifying the processor architecture.

Why are MEM_READ and MEM_WRITE separate signals?
Decision
Memory read and write operations are controlled using two independent one-bit signals.
Why?
The memory interface closely follows the classical datapath described in Hennessy and Patterson, where read and write operations are independently controlled. This makes the datapath easier to understand and simplifies the memory control logic for the supported instruction subset (LW and SW).

Why isn't IMM_SEL propagated beyond the Decode stage?
IMM_SEL is only required by the Immediate Generator to determine how the immediate field should be extracted from the instruction. Once the 32-bit immediate has been generated, the selection information has served its purpose and is no longer needed.
